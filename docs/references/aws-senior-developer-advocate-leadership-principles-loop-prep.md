- Official Amazon sources (Leadership Principles definitions, hiring guidance, and AWS Senior Developer Advocate job postings)

- Role-fit analysis for **AWS Senior Developer Advocate** (technical depth + community influence + product feedback loop)

## High-confidence role context (from official AWS/Amazon pages)

For Senior Developer Advocate roles, recurring expectations include:

- Create technical content (tutorials, code samples, workshops, thought leadership)
- Represent AWS publicly (talks, events, meetups, community channels)
- Engage developers in forums/social channels and build trust
- Influence product roadmap from field feedback
- Maintain hands-on coding credibility

These responsibilities strongly increase the probability of LP focus on customer empathy, communication, judgment, execution, and influence without authority.

## LPs you can de-prioritize (not ignore)

If you are short on prep time for this role, de-prioritize these first:

- **Hire and Develop the Best**
  - Why lower: more central to people-manager hiring loops than individual contributor DevRel loops.
- **Strive to Be Earth's Best Employer**
  - Why lower: usually less directly probed unless your stories involve team culture or mentorship at scale.
- **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility**
  - Why lower: tends to appear in senior product/strategy contexts; still possible if your talks/content had societal impact, compliance, safety, or responsible AI implications.
- **Frugality**
  - Why lower: usually appears as a supporting theme inside delivery/resource stories rather than as a primary probe.

Important: Do **not** fully exclude any LP. Amazon interviewers often distribute LP coverage across the loop.

## Detailed question bank by LP + what they assess

Use STAR. For each answer, include:

- scope (team/product/community size),
- your direct action,
- measurable outcome,
- what you learned and changed next time.

---

### 1) Customer Obsession

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a time developer feedback contradicted your product team's assumptions.
2. Describe when you delayed or changed a launch/content plan due to customer pain.
3. Give an example where your technical metrics looked good but user experience was poor.
4. Tell me about your toughest developer audience and how you adapted.
5. Describe a time you advocated for customer needs despite internal pressure.
6. Tell me about when you turned repeated community complaints into a concrete fix.

#### What they are assessing

- Do you start from developer/customer pain instead of internal preference?
- Can you distinguish vanity metrics from real customer outcomes?
- Will you escalate uncomfortable truths on behalf of users?
- Do you balance urgency with customer trust and long-term adoption?

---

### 2) Earn Trust

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a time you had to admit a mistake publicly (internally or externally).
2. Describe how you rebuilt trust after shipping misleading or incomplete technical content.
3. Give an example of partnering with skeptical engineering/product stakeholders.
4. Tell me about a disagreement with a community leader and how you handled it.
5. Describe a situation where you had to deliver bad news candidly.
6. Tell me how you build trust quickly in a new technical community.

#### What they are assessing

- Authenticity and humility under pressure
- Consistency between words and actions
- Candid communication without damaging relationships
- Ability to represent AWS credibly to technical audiences

---

### 3) Learn and Be Curious

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a time you had to become productive in a new domain fast (for example, GenAI/RAG/Bedrock).
2. Describe a technical area you self-learned and then taught at scale.
3. Give an example where curiosity changed your strategy or architecture recommendation.
4. Tell me about a wrong assumption you corrected through learning.
5. Describe how you stay current while balancing execution commitments.
6. Tell me about a learning investment that paid off for the business.

#### What they are assessing

- Learning velocity and depth
- Practical experimentation (not passive reading)
- Ability to convert learning into developer value
- Growth mindset and intellectual humility

---

### 4) Dive Deep

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a time you found root cause when metrics and anecdotal feedback conflicted.
2. Describe an incident where your low-level technical understanding changed a high-level decision.
3. Give an example of debugging a hard issue in a demo/sample/tutorial under time pressure.
4. Tell me about when you audited assumptions in your own content or architecture.
5. Describe the most technically complex system you had to explain clearly to developers.
6. Tell me about a case where you found an important detail others missed.

#### What they are assessing

- Technical depth and rigor
- Evidence-based reasoning
- Ability to move between details and narrative
- Reliability as a technical authority

---

### 5) Deliver Results

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a major content or community program you delivered with constrained time.
2. Describe a time you had to recover a slipping plan before a public deadline.
3. Give an example of balancing multiple launches/events and still hitting outcomes.
4. Tell me about a commitment you missed and how you recovered credibility.
5. Describe a time setbacks forced a pivot and you still delivered impact.
6. Tell me about the highest-impact DevRel result you produced and why it mattered.

#### What they are assessing

- Execution discipline under ambiguity
- Prioritization and tradeoff management
- Accountability for outcomes, not activity
- Resilience and recovery behavior

---

### 6) Ownership

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a problem outside your formal scope that you chose to own.
2. Describe when you drove cross-functional alignment without formal authority.
3. Give an example where short-term optics conflicted with long-term developer trust.
4. Tell me about taking responsibility for a failed initiative and fixing it.
5. Describe how you ensured continuity when dependencies were weak.
6. Tell me about creating mechanisms so success did not depend on heroics.

#### What they are assessing

- Long-term thinking and stewardship
- Bias toward accountable action
- End-to-end follow-through
- Company-first mindset beyond role boundaries

---

### 7) Are Right, A Lot

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a high-stakes technical judgment call you made with incomplete data.
2. Describe when you sought disconfirming evidence and changed your conclusion.
3. Give an example of incorporating diverse expert opinions into a better decision.
4. Tell me about a decision that proved wrong and what you changed in your approach.
5. Describe how you validated the right developer narrative before scaling it.
6. Tell me about a recommendation where your judgment improved business/customer outcomes.

#### What they are assessing

- Decision quality under uncertainty
- Intellectual honesty and calibration
- Use of multiple perspectives
- Strength of reasoning and postmortem learning

---

### 8) Invent and Simplify

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a complex workflow you simplified for developers.
2. Describe a novel content/demo format you introduced and its impact.
3. Give an example where simplification reduced adoption friction.
4. Tell me about an invention that failed and what you learned.
5. Describe when you reused external ideas instead of reinventing.
6. Tell me about reducing cognitive load in docs/workshops/samples.

#### What they are assessing

- Productive creativity (novel and useful)
- Simplicity as a force multiplier
- User empathy in design choices
- Ability to scale impact through better mechanisms

---

### 9) Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about respectfully challenging a senior stakeholder decision.
2. Describe a time you pushed back on a roadmap/content direction with evidence.
3. Give an example where you disagreed strongly, decision went the other way, and you fully committed.
4. Tell me about an unpopular position you defended for customer benefit.
5. Describe a difficult cross-team conflict and how you resolved it.
6. Tell me when not speaking up would have harmed outcomes.

#### What they are assessing

- Courage with professionalism
- Structured dissent (data + reasoning)
- Emotional maturity after decisions are made
- Alignment behavior in high-friction environments

---

### 10) Think Big

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a bold DevRel vision you proposed and executed.
2. Describe when you expanded a local initiative into a global or repeatable program.
3. Give an example of looking around corners and preparing for a platform shift.
4. Tell me about a strategic bet that changed adoption trajectory.
5. Describe your highest-leverage idea and measurable downstream effects.
6. Tell me about a time you under-scoped and what you changed.

#### What they are assessing

- Strategic imagination grounded in reality
- Ability to inspire others around a compelling direction
- System-level thinking beyond task completion
- Pattern recognition and future orientation

---

### 11) Insist on the Highest Standards

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about raising quality standards for technical content or demos.
2. Describe rejecting "good enough" and the cost/benefit of that decision.
3. Give an example of building review mechanisms to prevent recurring defects.
4. Tell me about setting quality bars for public speaking or workshop delivery.
5. Describe handling a teammate/stakeholder who wanted lower standards.
6. Tell me about a quality miss that led to new mechanisms.

#### What they are assessing

- Craft excellence and quality bar ownership
- Mechanism-building versus one-off fixes
- Willingness to defend quality under schedule pressure
- Long-term reliability mindset

---

### 12) Bias for Action

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about a reversible decision you made quickly that unlocked progress.
2. Describe when waiting for perfect data would have harmed outcomes.
3. Give an example of taking a calculated risk in a public-facing context.
4. Tell me about moving an initiative from idea to first customer value rapidly.
5. Describe a fast decision that failed and how you corrected course.
6. Tell me about removing organizational blockers proactively.

#### What they are assessing

- Speed with judgment (not recklessness)
- Risk framing and mitigation
- Momentum creation under ambiguity
- Learning loops after rapid action

---

### 13) Frugality (light prep)

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about delivering outsized impact with constrained budget/tools.
2. Describe replacing expensive process/tooling with a simpler mechanism.
3. Give an example of resource constraints driving a better design.
4. Tell me about consciously trading spend for speed or vice versa.

#### What they are assessing

- Resourcefulness and practicality
- Cost-awareness without quality collapse
- Ability to prioritize high-ROI actions

---

### 14) Hire and Develop the Best (light prep)

2. Describe raising the talent bar in a team/community context.
3. Give an example of coaching that changed someone's trajectory.

#### What they are assessing

### 15) Strive to Be Earth's Best Employer (light prep)

#### Questions to prepare

1. Tell me about improving inclusion/accessibility in your technical content.
2. Describe creating a psychologically safe environment in technical collaboration.
3. Give an example of supporting teammate growth and wellbeing while delivering.
4. Tell me about a mechanism you introduced to improve team experience.

#### What they are assessing

### 16) Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility (light prep)

#### Questions to prepare

2. Describe a decision where ethics/security/responsible AI changed your approach.
3. Give an example of balancing growth with ecosystem/community responsibility.
4. Tell me about improving guidance to prevent misuse or unsafe defaults.

#### What they are assessing

- Responsibility mindset at scale
- Ethical and societal awareness
- Long-horizon judgment beyond immediate KPIs

## Interviewer intent map (quick decoding)

When they ask behavioral questions, they are usually testing one or more of:

- **Judgment quality**: how you reason with incomplete data
- **Influence without authority**: critical in Developer Advocacy
- **Customer signal fidelity**: whether you truly represent developers
- **Technical credibility**: depth, precision, and ability to simplify
- **Execution reliability**: delivering visible outcomes repeatedly
- **Learning speed**: adapting as cloud/AI ecosystems shift

## Final prep checklist (for your loop)

- Prepare 2-3 STAR stories for each Tier 1 LP
- Prepare 1-2 stories for each Tier 2 LP
- Prepare 1 backup story each for Tier 3 LPs
- Ensure every story includes numbers (adoption, time saved, ticket reduction, reach, conversion)
- Practice concise first-pass answers (2-3 minutes), then deep-dive detail on follow-ups
- Build a story matrix so one story can map to multiple LPs without sounding rehearsed

## Suggested rehearsal order (highest ROI)

1. Customer Obsession + Earn Trust
2. Dive Deep + Learn and Be Curious
3. Ownership + Deliver Results
4. Are Right, A Lot + Invent and Simplify
5. Have Backbone + Think Big + Highest Standards
6. Bias for Action, then Tier 3 LPs
